How it's different from video lessons

Watching and reading help you understand technique. Here you also control the rider yourself: you try a move, see the result and can change something. Motor learning relies on this loop: action, feedback, another try. It helps you break a move down; you learn to do it with your body on the snow.

Shift your weight, change edges, bend your knees and see right away how the board reacts: where it turned, how speed and direction changed. You can slow the move down, try it in different ways and compare the result. The details get clearer: what to do, why, and how the body moves and the board reacts.

Who it's for

Beginners
Get to the mountain with less fear, already understanding the basics and the mechanics of riding, and knowing what to focus on in your body and movement.
Intermediate riders
Make sense of what you already do and spot your mistakes: wrong movements turn into habits, and we stop noticing them. Figure out linked turns and the basics of carving, and take a few runs in the simulator.
Short on time
Don't spend expensive hours with an instructor on basic theory.
